## PURPOSE
To frame a balanced scientific discussion on the role of LH activity in controlled ovarian
stimulation for women with **poor ovarian response (POR)**. This is background for scientific
exchange, not a promotional or prescribing recommendation. Treatment selection is an
individual clinical decision.

## SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT
POR is a heterogeneous population. It is defined by the ESHRE **Bologna criteria**
(Ferraretti et al., 2011) and stratified by the **POSEIDON criteria** (Alviggi et al., 2016).
Because aetiology and prognosis vary widely, no single stimulation approach is established as
optimal for all POR patients, and protocols remain individualised.

## MECHANISM (hypothesis-generating)
**Menotropin (Menopur, highly purified hMG)** contains both FSH and LH activity, the LH
activity being driven predominantly by hCG. Mechanistically, LH activity supports theca-cell
androgen production and follicular steroidogenesis, which *may* be relevant in patients with
low endogenous LH. Whether this mechanism translates into differences in clinical outcomes in
POR specifically is **not established** and remains an area of investigation. [Ref: reproductive
endocrinology mechanism review — to attach]

## WHAT THE EVIDENCE DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW
- Randomised data comparing hMG with recombinant FSH exist (e.g. **MERiT**, Andersen et al.,
  Hum Reprod 2006; **MEGASET**, Devroey et al., Fertil Steril 2012) and have been synthesised in
  Cochrane reviews of gonadotropins for ovarian stimulation.
- **Important limitation:** these pivotal comparisons were conducted largely in *normal*
  responders, not in a POR population. They therefore **cannot** be used to claim a live-birth
  benefit of menotropin specifically in poor responders.
- A definitive, adequately powered head-to-head RCT showing a live-birth advantage of
  menotropin over rFSH *in POR* is **not currently established**. Any efficacy discussion must
  state the population, endpoint, effect size with confidence interval, and source.

## SAFETY & FAIR BALANCE
- **OHSS:** Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome is a known risk of *all* gonadotropins, including
  menotropin. It is not avoided by any single product; risk is managed through patient
  selection, dosing, monitoring and protocol choice.
- **Other risks:** multiple pregnancy, injection-site reactions, hypersensitivity, and the
  thromboembolic risks associated with assisted reproductive technology (ART). Refer to the
  approved Prescribing Information (PI) / Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC).
- **Contraindications & full adverse-event profile:** per the current approved PI — to be
  appended in full for any externally shared version.

## BALANCED DISCUSSION POINTS (for reactive scientific exchange)
MSLs engage in **balanced, non-promotional** scientific exchange and do **not** recommend or
promote prescribing. Appropriate conduct:
- Respond to *unsolicited* clinical questions with the full data, including limitations and safety.
- Where a clinician raises LH activity in POR, present the mechanistic rationale **and** the gap
  in POR-specific outcome evidence.
- Always pair any efficacy data with proportionate safety information and the source reference.
- Defer prescribing and protocol decisions to the treating clinician's judgement and the
  approved indication.

## OPEN SCIENTIFIC QUESTIONS (advisory-board discussion)
1. In which POR sub-phenotypes (POSEIDON groups) might exogenous LH activity be most relevant?
2. What endpoints (live birth, cumulative live birth, oocyte yield) should a POR-specific trial use?
3. What is the current real-world experience among the advisory faculty?
